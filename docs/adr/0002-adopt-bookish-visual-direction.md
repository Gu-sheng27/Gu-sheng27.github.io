# Adopt the bookish visual direction

The site uses the bookish direction — one narrow column, serif-led typography, grouping by whitespace and type scale, no cards, no borders, no shadows — over the two alternatives we reviewed with live references and screenshots: terminal/geek (monospace, 1px rules, single accent) and modern product (geometric sans, radii, soft shadows, colour accents).

The deciding factor is durability rather than taste: typographic sites read well for a decade, terminal styling dates itself as an identity statement and fatigues by the third article, and product styling on a personal site tends to look like a template — which is exactly what building from scratch is meant to avoid.

Two elements are borrowed from the rejected directions rather than the whole language: the monospace face for meta information (dates, tags, reading time) comes from the terminal direction, and the two-layer dark-mode surface tokens and `--ring` focus system come from the product direction.

## Consequences

A distinct visual direction is cheap to state and expensive to change: once pages and content exist, reversing it means re-laying-out every template. This record exists so the plainness reads as a decision rather than as unfinished styling.
